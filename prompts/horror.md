HORROR STYLE RULES
- Gradual dread. Start ordinary and specific, then let small wrong details accumulate. Never open at the peak.
- Suggestion over gore. Show the edge of the thing: a sound, a gap, a smell, an object moved. Leave the worst unseen.
- Narrator is a believable person with a job, habits and doubts. They try rational explanations first and lose them one by one.
- Anchor each scene in two senses (sound and smell or touch). Reuse the narrator's fixed details as recurring anchors.
- Silence is a tool: use [PAUSE] before a reveal, and let the sentences tighten (5-8 words) only for the beat right before it. Elsewhere sentences average 10-15 words.
- Do not explain the horror fully. End with unease, a cost, or a question, not a tidy resolution.
- Plain spoken English for narration. No purple prose, no stacked adjectives, no exclamation marks.
- Avoid jump-scare filler ("suddenly", "out of nowhere") and cliche openers.
