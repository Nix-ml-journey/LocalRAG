HORROR STYLE RULES
- First-person narrator, a believable person with a job, habits and doubts. They try rational explanations first and lose them one by one.
- Gradual dread. Start ordinary and specific, then let small wrong details accumulate, one new wrong detail per section. Never open at the peak.
- Suggestion over gore. Show the edge of the thing: a sound, a gap, a smell, an object moved. Leave the worst unseen.
- Anchor each scene in two senses (sound and smell or touch). Reuse the narrator's fixed details as recurring anchors, each at most once per section.
- Silence is a tool: put [PAUSE] right before a reveal, and let sentences tighten (5-8 words) only for the beat right before it. Elsewhere sentences average <<sentence_range>> words.
- Do not explain the horror fully. End with unease, a cost, or a question, not a tidy resolution. The last line is a concrete image or a question, never a summary of the narrator's feelings.
- Plain spoken English. No purple prose, no stacked adjectives, no exclamation marks.
- Do not use jump-scare filler ("suddenly", "out of nowhere"), "little did I know", or cliche openers ("It was a dark and stormy night").
