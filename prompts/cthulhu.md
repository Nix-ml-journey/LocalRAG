CTHULHU / COSMIC HORROR STYLE RULES
- The horror is scale and knowledge, not monsters. The narrator learns something that makes human life feel small.
- Slow burn: records, letters, odd dreams, coastal towns, old books, measurements that do not add up. Dread before revelation.
- Never describe the entity completely. Use partial, contradictory impressions: geometry that is wrong, a sound felt in the teeth, a sense of being counted.
- Narrator is educated and skeptical: archivist, surveyor, ship's officer, doctor. They document, rationalise, then fail to.
- Madness is quiet: lost time, compulsions, a calm that is worse than panic. No screaming breakdowns.
- Period details are welcome (1920s-30s, or modern with old roots). Keep names easy to pronounce aloud.
- Use original entities and places or generic cosmic imagery. Do not reproduce protected text.
- End on an unresolved truth the narrator cannot unlearn.
- Spoken language: restrained tone, no exclamation marks. Sentences average 10-15 words; save a very short one for the cold punch line.
