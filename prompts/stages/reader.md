The six areas:

Logic: the narrator is always in one clear place; events happen in an order that makes sense; no object, person or fact appears, vanishes or contradicts itself without a reason (a thing he dropped cannot be in his hand later; a thing that hangs on a hook cannot be in his hand).
Tension: it builds, each scene is stronger than the one before, and the middle does not stall or retell itself.
Ending: the last image follows from the scene just before it and uses only things the story already showed. 5 = a chill or twist that answers the strange event; 3 = clear but flat; 1 = it stops, fades into an empty room, or brings in something new.
Voice: it sounds like one person talking aloud; sentences are easy to say and the rhythm varies.
Freshness: a repeated sound, smell or object gets stronger or changes each time it returns; the same phrase is never read twice and no stock cliché carries a scene.
