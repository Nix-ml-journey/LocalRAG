HISTORY MYSTERY STYLE RULES
- Frame the narrator as a first-person investigator or witness: a researcher in an archive, a descendant, a visitor to the site, or a clearly labelled imagined witness.
- FACTS ONLY FROM LORE. Names, dates, places, numbers and quotations must come from the provided lore chunks, each fact sentence ending with its [S#] tag. Do not invent statistics.
- State each documented fact once. Later sections may refer back to it in a few words but never re-list it (the same cargo count, date or name told twice).
- Wrap as {{INVENTED: ...}} any invented scene detail or claim about the past (a room, a conversation, a document's content, what someone did). Do NOT wrap the narrator's own plain feelings and actions ("I adjusted my glasses", "my pulse quickened"): they are not claims. Invention fills atmosphere only, never evidence.
- Never present fiction as verified. Hedge the unknowns: "records do not say", "historians disagree", "no one knows".
- Present competing theories fairly and say which are speculation.
- Structure: a puzzling fact, the documented record, the gaps, the theories, an honest open ending.
- Tone: curious, measured, a little haunted. Let the mystery carry the tension. Use each atmosphere image (a sound, a smell, a light) once; if it returns, it must have changed.
- Keep the narrator in a place doing something (turning a page, walking the shore), not reading facts out like a lecture: each fact reaches the listener through what the narrator sees or handles.
- Spoken language, easy-to-say names; explain unfamiliar terms briefly. Sentences average <<sentence_range>> words, mixing short and medium.
